\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{The conditional answer.} Given a correction that adds or retracts requirements, verifiers built without it accepted violations of it on the tasks with correction violators (CS-01, CS-03 and CS-04), and verifiers built with it did not, under labels that see only what the model-written reference checks exercise. That contrast follows largely from what the contracts of C1 and C3 contain (\S\ref{sec:results-planned}). The data are consistent with our hypothesis, descriptively, for correction-aware against initial-only reconstruction. They show no benefit of the audit, which added false rejections, and on false acceptance structured contracts did not differ from a session-LLM baseline. In the natural case studies (\S\ref{sec:natural-cases}), C1 to C4 and B2 rejected the pre-review state in NP-03, NP-04, NP-05, NP-18, NP-19 and NP-20, but these rejections inform only where the verifier passes the family's reference: C1 and C3 reject every candidate in NP-03, NP-05 and NP-18 (C4 too in NP-03), as C1 does in NP-20, and B1 passes the unchanged base, and with it the pre-review state, in NP-03, NP-05, NP-19 and NP-20. In NP-04, where C1 and C3 pass the reference, and in NP-19, the correction-aware verifiers accepted a near miss on the correction that the label rule marks invalid and that targets a correction requirement their contract does not cover (R5 in NP-04, R4 in NP-19). By the label rule's violated sets, C3, C4 and B2 also accept candidates that break a correction requirement this contract does cover, R4 in NP-04's eval-nm-3 and R3 in NP-19's eval-nm-1, so the coverage gap explains NP-04's eval-nm-2 alone. In NP-03, NP-04, NP-05, NP-18, NP-19 and NP-20, B2's false acceptance and false rejection are no higher than C3's or C4's, and C3's are no higher than C1's; both comparisons favour verifiers that agree with the addressing commit, on which the reference checks were validated.

\paragraph{The reference gate.} A gate that keeps a verifier only where it passes its own reference catches the loud inversion of CS-01, where C1 encodes the retracted request, and misses the silent loss of CS-03 and CS-04 (a post hoc reading of the secondary view, \S\ref{sec:results-planned}), where C1 passes its reference and is not asked to test what the correction added. A pipeline that gates its verifiers on the reference would therefore keep the second kind of error. In the natural arm the reference is the addressing commit, not an oracle, and a gate and \texttt{audit:v1} settle any disagreement between a reference and its contract in the reference's favour. The gated view drops C1 in NP-03, NP-05, NP-18, NP-19 and NP-20, C3 in NP-03, NP-05, NP-18 and NP-19, and C4 in NP-03 and NP-19. A request that the correction changed cannot explain the drops of the correction-aware verifiers, whose contracts include the correction. Where the decision log records why a reference fails its contracts, it records no retraction: before any grade, the generators of NP-03, NP-18 and NP-19 reported a content type left unconverted, a requirement's wording not met, or, in NP-19, a fix that does not take effect in the task's environment (D70). In NP-05, for which D70 gives no reason, requirements of both recovered contracts contradict the reference contract under both mappers. In NP-20, where C3 passes the reference and the gate drops C1 alone, the recovered contracts record a superseded request, as CS-01's scenario does (Table~\ref{tab:scenarios}): the correction-aware contract marks a requirement superseded, and a requirement of the initial-only contract contradicts the reference contract under both mappers (Table~\ref{tab:nr-coverage}), as requirements of CS-01's initial-only contract do (Table~\ref{tab:coverage}). D70 gives no reason for C1's drop in NP-20, so a retraction there is a reading of these records, not a recorded fact.

\paragraph{Whole-session generation.} Under these labels, generating from the whole session, through a contract (C3) or straight from the transcript (B2), rejected every correction violator of the controlled tasks. The natural arm qualifies this. Counted as the controlled tasks count them, as invalid candidates that break a requirement introduced at the correction, C3 and C4 accept correction violators in NP-04 (eval-nm-2 and eval-nm-3) and NP-19 (eval-nm-1), and B2 in NP-04 (eval-nm-3) and NP-19 (eval-nm-1); of these, only eval-nm-2 breaks no correction requirement that the correction-aware contract covers. An initial-only contract can also hold the correction already: CS-02's covers everything its correction adds, and NP-05's and NP-18's cover their correction requirements, so the initial-only arm measures what the proposer infers, not a fixed property of the session. The contracts' value over the session-LLM baseline is untested where it should matter: sessions with several corrections, or with corrections that conflict with each other, which our scenarios do not contain.

\paragraph{Agent-added tests cut both ways.} The tests the session agent added cause all \fv{b1.fr.added} of B1's false rejections, and they also reject every invalid attempt that the base tests alone would accept (\fv{b1.base.fa}: CS-03 \fv{b1.base.fa.cs03}, CS-04 \fv{b1.base.fa.cs04}). The base tests reject every CS-01 violator (\fv{b1.u1.base.rejected.cs01}), partly by construction: they are the label rule's regression norm, so under the label rule they cannot reject a valid attempt, and REGRESSION is in every CS-01 violator's violated set (Table~\ref{tab:fresh-behaviours}). If a pipeline takes a session's tests at the reference state as its verifier, keeping the agent's added tests apart from the repository's own would show which effect it gets.

\paragraph{What would change the conclusions.} None of the following has been done. Human labels from blinded reviewers, made with the existing review packet and reported beside the label rule's labels and the second labeler's, with their agreement; a labeler from another model family; a controlled expansion with more families drawn by a fixed procedure and a primary endpoint fixed in advance, the reference-gated C1--C3 difference in false acceptance; a second model family, and named models; baselines tied to practice: a first-request suite gated on the pre-correction state, a full-session task statement with test refinement, and an LLM judge; attempts without the build session's context (protocol~\S18.3); human coding of the S9 screening; and \texttt{natural-pr-v2}. Once human labels exist, the design to aim for combines frozen initial-only and correction-aware verifiers from the same procedure, held-out candidates from the sessions' own pre-correction states and from fresh attempts, and labels independent of both.
