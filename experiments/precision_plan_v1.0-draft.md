# Precision plan for the confirmatory study (v1.0, draft)

**Status: DRAFT.** This plan is not hashed, not logged and not in force. The coordinator
hashes it and records it in the decision log before it governs anything; until then
protocol v0.7 governs. It was written on 2026-11-21, after `pilot-v0.3.3` and its post
hoc, exploratory re-analysis had been seen, so every number taken from the pilot is a
planning input, not evidence. It drafts what protocol §9 leaves to v1.0: the analysis
plan, the number of task families, justified by the pilot's variability, and the
annotation effort.

**Sources.** Every number comes from one of two files in
`results/analyses/pilot-v0.3.3-exploratory/`, regenerated by
`python scripts/exploratory_reanalysis.py` (seed 20261121, about 90 seconds):
* `planning_simulation.json`, cited as `sim:` with its JSON path;
* `analysis.json`, cited as `ana:`.

The tables under `paper/generated/exploratory/planning_*.tex` show the same values, and
`paper/evidence_ledger_exploratory.md` lists each of them with its path.

## 1. Units, populations and labels

* **Unit.** The task family: one source session or pull request, with everything derived
  from it. All inference is over families. Candidates are never treated as independent.
* **Confirmatory families.** New families only.
  * CS-01 to CS-04 are development families (protocol §15.1), and so is any family used to
    develop or tune a recipe.
  * Eligible: natural PR-review families (`natural-pr-v1` and later natural strata) and
    controlled families from a separately hashed expansion.
* **Strata.** The primary analysis pools eligible families across confirmatory strata and
  says so. Each stratum's estimate is reported beside the pooled one.
* **Labels.**
  * The confirmatory analysis uses human labels: two reviewers per family, with
    disagreements adjudicated (§14).
  * Rule D19 and the independent labeler (§15.5) are reported as secondary labelings.
  * Without human labels the confirmatory analysis is not run.
* **Duplicates.** Each distinct graded patch counts once within a family (§15.7).

## 2. Primary endpoint: the paired FA difference C1 vs C3 on natural negatives

* **Negatives.** Candidates that were not built to fail and that the human labels call
  invalid. Their origin is `observed_pre_correction`, `natural_pre_review` or
  `fresh_attempt`. Constructed near misses are excluded from the primary endpoint.
* **Eligible family.** A family with at least one such negative. Ineligible families are
  counted and reported, with the reason.
* **Per family.** d_f = FA(C1) − FA(C3) over the family's eligible negatives, where C1 is
  `initial_plain` and C3 is `full_plain`.
* **Estimand.** δ, the mean of d_f over eligible families: each family weighs the same. The
  pooled difference is reported as a secondary estimate.
* **Hypotheses.** H0: δ ≤ 0 against H1: δ > 0, at one-sided α = 0.025.
* **Test.**
  * The exact sign-flip randomization test of the sum of d_f over all 2^K sign assignments,
    computed exactly (as `signflip` in `scripts/exploratory_reanalysis.py`).
  * Under H0, with the two verifiers exchangeable within a family, the test is exact for any
    number of families.
* **Interval.** The two-sided 95% interval from inverting the test: the values δ0 that
  neither one-sided test of d_f − δ0 rejects at 0.025. Its coverage rests on approximate
  symmetry of d_f, and §6 checks it.
* **Display.** The per-family table comes first (§15.7). Bootstrap intervals go to an
  appendix, marked as not inferential below 10 families.
* **The pilot, post hoc.**
  * 2 of 4 families have an invalid pre-correction state (`ana: natural_negatives`).
  * Both have d_f = 1, so the one-sided p is 0.25, the floor for two families
    (`sim: pilot_inputs.natural_negatives`).

## 3. FR guardrail (non-inferiority)

* **Margin: +0.10 in FR (absolute).** That is one valid solution in ten.
  * A larger margin would let a verifier reject valid alternatives routinely.
  * A smaller one needs more valid candidates per family than the recipe can supply (§8).
* **Hypotheses.** H0: FR(C3) − FR(C1) ≥ 0.10 against H1: FR(C3) − FR(C1) < 0.10, at
  one-sided α = 0.025.
* **Method (conservative).**
  * FR(C1) ≥ 0, so FR(C3) < 0.10 implies that the difference is below the margin. The
    guardrail is therefore tested on FR(C3) alone.
  * The bound is the one-sided 97.5% Clopper-Pearson upper bound of C3's pooled FR. It is
    computed on the Kish effective sample size n/DEFF, with DEFF = 1 + (m̄ − 1)ρ and ρ = 0.3.
    False rejections are divided by DEFF and rounded up; n/DEFF is rounded down.
  * The guardrail is met if the bound is below 0.10.
* **Why not the shifted sign-flip test.**
  * At the boundary (true FR 0.12 for C3 against 0.02 for C1), it rejects in 0.03 to 0.11
    of replicates, against the nominal 0.025 (`sim: fr_guardrail.signflip_shifted`).
  * False rejections are rare, so the shifted per-family differences are skewed, and the
    test is anti-conservative.
* **Size of the bound** at a true FR(C3) of 0.10 (`sim: fr_guardrail.deff_bound`):
  * at most 0.011 when the true ICC is 0 or 0.3;
  * up to 0.035 when it is 0.6.
* **The ICC assumption matters.** The following are therefore reported beside it:
  * the realized ICC (ANOVA estimate over families);
  * the guardrail recomputed with the realized ICC;
  * the assumption-free bound from one valid candidate per family, chosen at random before
    grading (Clopper-Pearson over families). With no false rejection it needs at least 36
    families (`sim: fr_guardrail.one_per_family_bound`).

## 4. B2 vs C3/C4 as an equivalence question

* **Question.** Is the full-session LLM baseline B2 within ±0.10 of the correction-aware
  verifiers in FA? In the pilot it differs from C3 and C4 by one candidate
  (`ana: b2_equivalence`), which is not evidence of equivalence.
* **Margin.** ±0.10 in FA (absolute). FR is a secondary outcome, with the same margin.
* **Population.** All candidates labeled invalid, constructed near misses included,
  because the question is about the verifiers, not about natural negatives. Each distinct
  graded patch counts once.
* **Test.**
  * Two one-sided shifted sign-flip tests over families (TOST), each at one-sided 0.025.
  * Equivalence is concluded if both reject; equivalently, the inverted 95% interval lies
    inside ±0.10.
  * The test is run for B2 vs C4 and for B2 vs C3.
* **Size at the margin** (FA 0.15 against 0.05, between-family SD 0.05): 0.008 to 0.029
  against the nominal 0.025, within Monte Carlo error (2000 replicates, standard error
  about 0.0035; `sim: equivalence.cells`). The same kind of shifted test is
  anti-conservative in the FR scenarios of §3, which have rarer events and more clustering.
  Before H3 and H4 are tested, their size is therefore simulated again at the observed C3
  rate and between-family SD. If it exceeds 0.03, H3 and H4 are reported descriptively
  only.
* **Power at equal rates (0.05):**
  * 0.89 with 40 families and 4 negatives each;
  * 0.81 with 60 families and 2 negatives each;
  * 0.47 with 20 families and 4 negatives each.
* **In the pilot.** With four families no TOST p below 1/16 is attainable
  (`ana: b2_equivalence.floor_one_sided`).

## 5. Multiplicity

* **Confirmatory hypotheses, in a fixed sequence.** Each is tested at one-sided α = 0.025
  only if every earlier one was rejected. Testing stops at the first non-rejection, so the
  family-wise error rate stays at 0.025 (one-sided).
  1. H1, the primary hypothesis: δ > 0 (§2).
  2. H2, the FR guardrail (§3).
  3. H3: B2 is equivalent to C4 in FA (§4).
  4. H4: B2 is equivalent to C3 in FA.
* **Why this order.**
  * H1 is the claim.
  * H2 is the condition for reading H1 as an improvement.
  * H3 and H4 ask whether the contract machinery adds anything over a competent full-session
    LLM.
  * A hypothesis that is not reached is reported descriptively, never as rejected.
* **Secondary analyses.** These are:
  * the other comparisons of protocol §7;
  * FR comparisons other than H2;
  * each stratum;
  * each secondary labeling;
  * the views of §15.7 (oracle-gated, distinct patches).

  They carry exact sign-flip p-values, Holm-adjusted within this secondary set, and are
  labeled descriptive. No confirmatory claim rests on them.
* **Exploratory views.** The right-reason view and the bounds over ambiguous labels are
  reported unadjusted and labeled exploratory.

## 6. An analysis valid with few clusters, and its coverage

* **Choice: the exact sign-flip test over families (§2), not a binomial GLMM.**
  * A GLMM with family random effects would need software outside the project's
    dependencies.
  * With few families, likelihood-based GLMM intervals have their own small-sample
    problems.
  * The sign-flip test needs only symmetry under H0, and it is exact for any number of
    families.
* **The coverage check** (`sim: coverage`):
  * 2000 replicates per cell and 400 bootstrap resamples;
  * per family, each verifier's acceptance probability is an independent Beta draw, and
    candidates are Bernoulli given it.

  It compares the inverted sign-flip interval with the cluster-bootstrap percentile
  interval that the pilot reports. The table gives the coverage of the nominal 95% interval
  for K families:

  | Scenario | Method | K = 4 | K = 6 | K = 8 | K = 12 | K = 20 |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Δ = 0.20, τ = 0.29, 2 negatives per family | sign-flip | (0.794 at 87.5%) | 0.918 | 0.954 | 0.951 | 0.946 |
  | | cluster bootstrap | 0.766 | 0.874 | 0.849 | 0.902 | 0.939 |
  | Δ = 0.35, τ = 0.47 (capped), 4 negatives per family | sign-flip | (0.850 at 87.5%) | 0.950 | 0.970 | 0.954 | 0.944 |
  | | cluster bootstrap | 0.840 | 0.890 | 0.904 | 0.928 | 0.927 |
  | null, both arms at 0.05, 2 negatives per family | sign-flip | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 0.998 |
  | | cluster bootstrap | 0.995 | 0.982 | 0.981 | 0.977 | 0.966 |

* **Reading.**
  * From 8 families on, the inverted sign-flip interval covers 0.944 to 0.970 in the two
    scenarios with an effect (nominal 0.95). At 6 families one of them gives 0.918.
  * Under the null with rare events the sign-flip interval is conservative (0.998 to 1.000).
  * With 4 families no test can reject at 0.025, so no finite 95% interval exists (its
    coverage of 1.000 is trivial). At the attainable 87.5% level the interval covers 0.79
    to 0.85 of the time.
  * In both scenarios with an effect, the cluster bootstrap under-covers at every K up to
    20, between 0.77 and 0.94.
* **Consequence.** The interval needs at least 8 eligible families. Power (§7) needs many
  more.

## 7. Number of families

### Input from the pilot

* The per-family C1 − C3 FA differences, on all negatives with D19 labels, are 0.17, 0.00,
  0.50 and 0.80: mean 0.37, SD 0.356 (`sim: pilot_inputs`).
* Removing the mean binomial variance (0.045) leaves a between-family SD of the true
  differences of τ = 0.29.
* The SD has 3 degrees of freedom. Its chi-square interval (10th to 90th percentile of the
  observed SD: 0.25 to 0.81) gives τ = 0.13 (low) and 0.78 (high).
* The high value is infeasible for proportions. It is capped at 0.34, 0.41 and 0.47 for
  Δ = 0.10, 0.20 and 0.35 (`sim: family_counts.tau_used`).

### The pool composition was designed

* Each pilot family has two valid alternatives and five near misses, most of them built to
  violate the correction, plus the observed pre-correction state.
* 16 of the 17 invalid candidates violate a correction requirement, largely by
  construction.
* The between-family SD therefore describes designed pools in four scripted sessions, not
  natural variation, and it is used here only as a planning input.
* The pilot has two natural negatives, too few to estimate any SD for the primary endpoint
  itself.

### Further assumptions

* Families are independent.
* Per family, each verifier's acceptance probability is a Beta draw:
  * C3 has mean 0.05 and SD 0.05. This is pessimistic against the pilot's 0/17, whose exact
    upper bound is 0.20.
  * C1 has mean 0.05 + Δ, with the SD that gives τ.
* Candidates are Bernoulli given their family.
* The number n of natural negatives per family is fixed at 1, 2 or 4.
* Δ = 0.35 is close to the pilot's estimate on all negatives and is probably optimistic.
  0.20 and 0.10 are planning values.
* There are 1000 replicates per cell, so the power standard error is at most 0.016. The
  level is one-sided α = 0.025.

* **Grid values.** Each entry is the first grid point that reaches the power, so the true
  minimum lies between the previous grid point and this one.
* **One negative per family.** The SD does not matter: the test becomes a sign test on the
  families where C1 and C3 disagree.
* **Screening.**
  * Eligible families must be divided by the share of screened families that yield a
    natural negative.
  * In the pilot, 2 of 4 pre-correction states were invalid (exact 95% interval 0.07 to
    0.93; `sim: pilot_inputs.natural_negatives`).

    families at a share of 0.8 and 120 at 0.5
    (`sim: family_counts.screened_families_for_power80`).

### Draft recommendation

* Plan for a smallest effect of interest Δ = 0.20. That means about 60 eligible families
  with one natural negative each, or 40 to 50 with two (for example the pre-review state
  plus one negative from a fresh-agent attempt).
* §15.6 caps `natural-pr-v1` at 30 tasks. With one negative per family, 30 families give
  power 0.45 at Δ = 0.20 and 0.87 at Δ = 0.35 (`sim: family_counts.power.n_neg=1`). The cap
  therefore supports only a large effect.
* Raising the cap, or adding natural negatives per family, is the owner's decision.

## 8. Valid candidates per family for the FR bound

* **Rule (§3).** The bound on C3's FR, on the effective sample size, is below 0.10.
* **Valid candidates per family needed**, when 0 / 1 false rejections are observed in all
  (`sim: fr_guardrail.valid_per_family`; "–": not reachable with up to 40 per family):

  | ICC | K = 10 | K = 15 | K = 20 | K = 30 | K = 40 | K = 60 |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | 0.00 | 4 / 6 | 3 / 4 | 2 / 3 | 2 / 2 | 1 / 2 | 1 / 1 |
  | 0.10 | 6 / 11 | 3 / 6 | 2 / 4 | 2 / 2 | 1 / 2 | 1 / 1 |
  | 0.30 | – / – | 6 / – | 3 / 10 | 2 / 3 | 1 / 2 | 1 / 1 |
  | 0.50 | – / – | – / – | 9 / – | 2 / 9 | 1 / 3 | 1 / 1 |

* **Power of the guardrail** when C3's true FR is 0.02 and the ICC is 0.3
  (`sim: fr_guardrail.deff_bound.icc_true=30pct`), with 2 / 3 / 4 valid candidates per
  family:
  * 40 families: 0.54 / 0.74 / 0.63;
  * 30 families: 0.36 / 0.50 / 0.41;
  * 20 families: 0.00 / 0.41 / 0.34.

  The bound is discrete. With 4 per family the extra expected false rejections cost more
  than the larger effective sample gains.
* **Draft recommendation.**
  * 3 valid candidates per family: one more than the two alternatives of the current
    recipe (the reference solution does not count).
  * At least 30 to 40 families.
  * With 2 per family and 20 families, the guardrail cannot be met at ICC 0.3 even with no
    false rejection.

## 9. Annotation hours from the review-packet sizes

* **Packet sizes** (`sim: annotation_hours.packet_sizes`), computed with the packet
  builder's own functions (`tracecontract.review`). Per task:
  * 7 to 8 distinct items;
  * 240 to 1484 changed diff lines (83 per item on average);
  * 177 to 261 words of user turns;
  * 4 to 6 reference requirements.
* **Assumed times**, low / central / high. These are assumptions, not measurements
  (`sim: annotation_hours.assumptions_minutes`):
  * setup: 10 / 15 / 30 minutes per task;
  * reading: user turns at 200 / 150 / 100 words per minute, the contract at 150 / 100 / 70;
  * contract review: 2 / 3 / 5 minutes per requirement decision;
  * per item: 6 / 10 / 20 minutes, plus 2 / 5 / 10 minutes in the sandbox, plus 0.1 / 0.2 /
    0.4 minutes per changed line;
  * two reviewers per task;
  * adjudication of 10 / 20 / 35% of items, at 10 / 15 / 25 minutes each.
* **The pilot's packet** (4 families, 31 items): 20.5 / 38.9 / 78.2 person-hours
  (`sim: annotation_hours.pilot_total_hours`). CS-02, with large diffs, takes 15.6 hours
  (central).
* **Per confirmatory family** (`sim: annotation_hours.projection`):
  * 4.1 / 7.8 / 15.6 hours with 6 items: the `natural-pr-v1` pool of the pre-review state,
    two alternatives and three near misses;
  * 5.3 / 10.0 / 20.1 hours with 8 items.
* **In total, with 6 items per family:**
  * 40 families: 166 / 312 / 624 person-hours;
  * 60 families: 249 / 468 / 937 person-hours.

  A third valid alternative (§8) adds about one item per family.
* **Before relying on these figures,** record the minutes spent per item in the first two
  reviews and replace the assumptions. The label template has no time field yet.
